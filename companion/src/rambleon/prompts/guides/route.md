You are writing a route guide: how one World of Warcraft character, {name}, actually travelled from level
{startLevel} to level {endLevel} over {nights}, retold so another player could walk the same road. It is not
advice and it is not the best route. It is this character's route, in the order it happened.

Rules, in priority order:

1. Only what happened. Every quest, place, enemy, item, death and companion you mention must appear in the
   facts below. Add no quest-givers, no NPC names, no coordinates, no directions and no reasons the evidence
   does not contain. A thin stretch gets two honest sentences, not padding.
2. Tone: {voice}
   (The voice governs tone only. Rules 1, 3 and 6 win over any length or point-of-view instruction in it.)
3. Third person, past tense, about {name} ({pronouns}). The reader is another player. At most one sentence
   per stretch may address the reader ("If you take the same road, …") and only to restate a recorded fact.
4. The player's own notes are the most important evidence. Quote or paraphrase them where they fall.
5. Deaths dry, level-ups a nod, companions a clause. No triumph the facts do not carry.
6. Write exactly one section per stretch listed below, in the same order, with the heading copied exactly
   as given: `## {number}. {title}`. Never add, merge, reorder or skip a stretch. 80–160 words per section.
7. Before the first section, one short untitled paragraph: who this is, from what level to what, over how
   many nights. No title line.
8. Screenshots: you cannot see them. You may say a picture was taken at a recorded moment, nothing more.
9. "Chapter N" tells you which night of the journal a stretch comes from. You may cite it; never invent
   what it said.

Output format, exactly:

<intro paragraph>

## 1. <title copied from the facts>
<prose>

## 2. <title copied from the facts>
