Chapter 16 — The Long Dig at Whelgar's

Rambleon Birdsong · October 7, 2026 · 2h 55m in Azeroth
StoryRecapQuestsJourney
← Chapter 15 — The Long Road to the Cathedral

Rambleon found Cassidy waiting for him almost before he'd stepped free of the Cathedral of Light, the thread between them, as he'd known the night before, not yet come undone. Together they made the familiar road down to Deepwater Tavern and out through Menethil Harbor into the marshes, and it was there, among crocolisks and fenrunners in Bluegill Marsh, that he passed into his twenty-fourth year of life — a small bright moment he paused to mark with a picture before the ooze and shadow of Ironbeard's Tomb swallowed the rest of the thought.

The real labor of the night waited at Whelgar's Excavation Site, where Tarrel Rockweaver's work seemed to multiply the longer Rambleon stayed: fragments to recover, a keystone to puzzle out, one task opening onto the next like nested boxes — Uncovering the Past, Understanding Our Present, Gleaning Our Future. Annastasia, a mage, fell in for the better part of an hour as raptors and screechers gave ground in the Black Channel Marsh, and Gurney, a druid, stayed near as long while the old bones of the dig yielded an Excavation Cloak and a Silk Mantle worth keeping. Natcho, the hunter he'd hunted beside the night before, turned up again for a short while among the mottled things, and Lanfair and Nawan, druid and hunter, passed through for a few minutes each, there and gone before he'd properly learned their faces.

The ground at Whelgar's was not kind to him, nor was Thelgen Rock later, nor Ironbeard's Tomb before either — he walked back from the graveyard more than once through the night, each time picking up the thread exactly where it had dropped. At Thelgen Rock he fell in with Uncle and Artemis, hunters both, and Cdna, a warrior, long enough to put down cave stalkers and leech stalkers and see the uprising there quelled, a Fortified Cloak of Shadow Wrath the reward for the trouble.

By the time Menethil Harbor was behind him again, the night had turned toward Ironforge, and Cassidy came the whole way with him — through Dun Morogh's cold and the Gates, into the warmth of the Great Forge and the quiet halls beyond, Tinker Town and the Vault and the High Seat all visited in turn, new tasks taken up at every stop. He stopped to mark the crossing into Dun Morogh, and again at the gates of the mountain city, two more pictures to set beside the one from Bluegill Marsh. It had been, he thought, closing on midnight, a night that never once asked him to walk it alone.
