Chapter 13 — Scales and Solitude

Rambleon Birdsong · October 4, 2026 · 2h 14m in Azeroth
StoryRecapQuestsJourney

Rambleon began the evening in Astranaar, as he so often did, but whatever the Gold Dust Exchange had wanted of him, he let it go unfinished and turned away instead. Alone this time — no Cassidy at his shoulder, no Gabe or Night crossing his path — he struck out along the coast he knew well, past the Shrine of Aessina, along Lake Falathim and the Zoram Strand, the Veiled Sea murmuring beside him, until the land rose into Stonetalon. A picture was taken at the border, the mountains opening ahead of him the way they always did, half promise and half warning.

The Stonetalon work kept him busy for the better part of an hour: Stonetalon Peak, then down to Mirkfallon Lake where the basilisks lurked thick in the shallows, and on into the Charred Vale, where harpies and windcallers and a lone ancient fell to his bow alongside more of the same scaled things. By the time he counted eight Crystalized Scales in his pack, the errand for Mauren was as good as done, though it would be another hour and a long road before he could say so properly.

That road ran back through Astranaar, past Maestra's Post, and down into Twilight Vale — Darkshore again, the ground familiar from nights not long past. He marked the crossing with another picture before threading the Long Wash and Auberdine's edge toward the Veiled Sea, then on to Stormwind Harbor, a third photograph taken as the city's towers rose before him. It was in the Mage Quarter, of all places, that Mauren's business finally closed, and a Spellcrafter Wand came into his hands for the trouble. He lingered in Stormwind longer than he meant to — the Canals, the Trade District, an idle hour in the Valley of Heroes — and came away from it with a Blackwater Cutlass besides, its edge humming faintly with a worth he hadn't expected from an ordinary evening's wandering.

The last of the night carried him home to Ashenvale, a wildthorn stalker falling near Raynewood Retreat before the dark of Nightsong Woods swallowed the path ahead. He walked it without company, and felt the difference of it — not loneliness exactly, but the particular quiet of ground usually shared.
