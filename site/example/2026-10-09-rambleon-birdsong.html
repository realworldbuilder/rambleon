Chapter 18 — The Vale That Wouldn't Let Go

Rambleon came down from the Deepwater Tavern's lamplight the same way he'd left it, alone again, and let the road carry him further than usual before supper. Theramore's walls passed in a glimpse, then the Barrens' dust, Mulgore's wells, the dry hush of Desolace — each new horizon worth a picture, though he didn't linger in any of them. Stonetalon gave way to Ashenvale, Ashenvale to Darkshore, and the ferry at Rut'theran carried him home to Teldrassil and up, at last, to Darnassus. In the Temple of the Moon he stood still long enough to mark the moment, and later, back at the Veiled Sea where the ferry waited, he marked another — the kind of quiet a Night Elf keeps for himself on a long night of travel.

But it was Stonetalon that pulled him back, and the Charred Vale in particular, where a Bloodfury windcaller and a storm witch and their roguefeathers and slayers had been left half-finished the night before. It had been sitting wrong with him — "leaving that charred vale quest open was bothering me," he admitted to himself somewhere on the path back — so he went in without Stoneheart or Falcon or anyone else to watch his back, and worked through it alone: ambushers, a charred ancient, a blackened basilisk, an antlered courser and a harpy besides, until the roguefeathers and slayers were accounted for six and six. The vale cost him twice before it gave up its answer, and once more, later, in Nightsong Woods — the dry fact of a death is all the night records, and all it need say.

Reclaiming the Charred Vale closed out at Stonetalon Peak, a small relief more than a triumph, and Rambleon turned east again into Ashenvale with lighter shoulders. Astranaar gave him Shindrell's note to carry to Kayneth Stillwind, and from Raynewood Retreat into Nightsong Woods he went about Raene's business — a venomspitter, an old bear, withered and crazed ancients, a ghostpaw alpha, and the iron shaft the cleansing called for — until the moonwell outside Astranaar finally took the offering from his hands.

He came back into Astranaar afterward and paused to mark it, a small ending to a long wandering night. Looking for somewhere to sit and rest, he found nothing. "No bars in Astranaar," he noted, not quite a complaint, and settled for the moonlight instead.
