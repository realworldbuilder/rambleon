Chapter 7 — What Darkshore Still Held

Rambleon Birdsong · September 27, 2026 · 1h 10m in Azeroth
StoryRecapQuestsJourney
← Chapter 6 — Across the Border to Ashenvale

Rambleon had barely settled into Astranaar, still getting the measure of Ashenvale's edges, when Cassidy found him again — as easy and familiar as ever, as though no time at all had passed since Blackwood Den had scattered them the night before. They did not linger in the new land. Something pulled them back west, through Maestra's Post and out along the border, and before long the trees changed and the air of Darkshore settled around him once more: Twilight Vale, Twilight Shore, the Long Wash, ground he had walked raw only a night ago. He paused near the boundary long enough to let a picture keep the moment, the way one does at a threshold worth remembering.

Auberdine again, then Bashal'Aran, then down along the Cliffspring, its river and its falls and the winding path between them — country that no longer asked anything new of him, only more of the same patient walking. A moonstalker fell to his bow along the water, and near the ruins a blackwood warrior went down as well. In Bashal'Aran's shadow a den mother met the same end. None of it was hard-won in the way the mountain had been the night before; it read more like tidying, the kind of quiet work that fills an hour without asking to be remembered as anything grander.

A priest named Tina joined them for a few minutes along the Cliffspring, the two of them falling into step easily enough before the moment passed and she went her own way — brief, unremarkable, the sort of company that comes and goes on any given afternoon in Darkshore. Cassidy stayed a while longer, familiar company doing what familiar company does, but by early evening she too parted ways with him near Auberdine, leaving Rambleon to consider that the night had given him little beyond motion and small kills — no new ground broken, no rank gained, nothing dramatic to set down. Seventeenth level still sat comfortably on him, unchallenged. It was, he thought, watching Auberdine's lamps in the dimming light, a fair kind of evening: not every hour in Azeroth needed to change a man to be worth living.
