Chapter 1 — First Winds Over Zephras

Winterland Dew · September 27, 2026 · 46m in Azeroth
StoryRecapQuestsJourney

Winterland Dew came ashore at Thendal Village with nothing yet to her name but a bow and a windshaper's restless curiosity. Zephras Isle greeted her plainly enough — a village, a grove pressing close beyond it, the sky doing whatever skies did on islands built half of stone and half of wind. She did not know yet what that meant. She would learn.

The grove taught her first. Juvenile vuldren, wary and quick, fell to her arrows one after another until the counting was done, and something in her settled and sharpened at the same moment — the particular click of a girl becoming, however slightly, a huntress. She felt the second level arrive like a held breath let out. Cirrusflies came after, pesky and stubborn, and then the standing stones drew her onward toward converts of the Al'Aketh and things called Roiling Winds, which she put down without ceremony and without quite understanding what they had been. A third level came to her in that circle of old stones, and she paused long enough to let a picture be taken, as if the moment deserved remembering before she moved on.

There was a note to find and a woman named Tai'ree Farsight to speak with in the grove — the Way of the Hunter, they called it, and it passed quickly, more instruction than trial. She viewed an Anchor Pylon at someone's asking and did not linger to wonder at it. Rorian sent her out and welcomed her back. A cirrusfly queen, guarded by her soldiers, met the same end as her subjects, and Winterland found herself standing over the small wreckage of that little war feeling, oddly, more curious than triumphant. Near an Elemental Convergence she used something called Skysight for the first time, and whatever it showed her, it was enough to satisfy the task. By the time she returned to the village a fourth level had found her, and Aetheen of the Gales gave her a task and took it back again almost in the same breath — brisk island business, nothing wasted.

She had walked alone the whole of it — no companion's voice at her shoulder, no one to share the small satisfaction of a queen fallen or a fourth level won. That was Zephras Isle for her tonight: a place that asked little conversation of her and gave her, instead, arrows spent, quests closed one after another like doors, and the beginning of whatever a windshaper was meant to become. Forty-six minutes was not long. It was long enough to stop being new.
