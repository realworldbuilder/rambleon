Chapter 24 — New Shores

Rambleon found Cassidy waiting for him again at Southshore, the way she so often did now, and they fell into step without ceremony — the same ease that had carried them from Aldrassil through a dozen nights since. Hillsbrad gave up its Snapjaws easily enough, and deeper in the grass a Vicious Gray Bear and a lone Elder Moss Creeper fell to the two of them before the coast called them onward, toward Menethil Harbor and the gray swells of the Great Sea.

The Wetlands opened around them familiar and green — Baradin Bay, Bluegill Marsh, Sundown Marsh, the reeds of Saltspray Glen thick with Highland Scytheclaws and Razormaws that seemed to breed faster than they could be thinned. An Elder Razormaw went down near the bay, and a Fen Creeper or eight besides, the kind of long unhurried grinding that asks nothing of a hunter but patience and a full quiver. He paused at Ironbeard's Tomb only long enough to look, and took a picture crossing back into those waters, the moment worth keeping even if the place was not new.

The road home to Hillsbrad bent instead toward something Rambleon had never seen — Darkshore, pale and unfamiliar under its own sky, and he marked the crossing with a photograph before Auberdine's lantern-lit quiet gave him a small finished thing to carry: Bloom of the Heavens, done. A druid named Ion joined him there for the span of a single minute, trading nothing more than company before moving on his own way. At the Tower of Althalaxx a Voidwalker Minion met its end in the ruins' shadow, and not long after, Cassidy parted from him for the night, the second time the two had unknotted and rejoined that evening, this time for good — Darkshore's quiet suddenly a little quieter.

He went on alone from there, as he had so often before when the company thinned, and the aloneness this time led somewhere entirely new. Ashenvale received him at the Zoram Strand, and he took a picture of that first step into trees he had never walked beneath. Lake Falathim, the Shrine of Aessina, the lamps of Astranaar, Raynewood Retreat, Nightsong Woods, the Falfarren River, Silverwing Outpost, Fallen Sky Lake, Silverwing Grove — one unfamiliar name folding into the next, a long green unspooling of country that asked only to be walked, and Rambleon walked it, level twenty-nine still settling comfortably on his shoulders, no closer to thirty but no poorer for the distance covered.
