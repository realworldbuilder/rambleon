Chapter 11 — Into the Deadmines

Rambleon left Astranaar before the lamplight had fully settled over it, down through Ashenvale and Darkshore and across the Veiled Sea to Stormwind Harbor, the long low road of Elwynn carrying him quickly south into Westfall's flat gold fields. It was a different country than the one that had held him the night before — no Charred Vale calling him back this time, only Sentinel Hill's dust and the Defias trouble that seemed to breed there without end. Cassidy found him soon enough, as she always did now, seven nights running since Aldrassil, and together they worked north through Moonbrook and the Dust Plains and into the Dagger Hills, pillagers and knuckledusters and highwaymen falling in a steady, unglamorous rhythm he'd come to know.

By evening the road led down into the Deadmines, and the company around him grew the way it sometimes did in a place that drew everyone's attention at once — Cortonna crossed his path only a minute before moving on, but Dire stayed the better part of an hour, Jstermy near as long, and Emchetyre close behind them, five of them pressing together through Defias diggers and miners and overseers, through the Mast Room and Sneed's circle, into the Goblin Foundry where the craftsmen and engineers kept them all busy, and finally to Gilnid himself. He fell, and not long after, in the gold light of Ironclad Cove, Rambleon crossed into his twenty-first level — a small, private satisfaction, the kind that came and went between one swing of the axe and the next. The mines took their toll twice that night, once in the Mast Room and once in the Cove, and both times the graveyard at Sentinel Hill knew his step before the fight found him again.

Above ground once more, he and Cassidy turned to the Dead Acre, reapers and fleshripper falling under a sky he never did describe, and Westfall's old troubles came round full circle — Gryan Stoutmantle's report delivered, the Red Leather Bandanas finished, the Killing Fields closed out twenty watchers deep, a cloak and a pair of worn leather trousers to show for it. Raven, a warlock new to him, joined for a stretch of that cleanup, and a stray note found on the road saw him sent north again.

The last hours ran long and quiet through familiar country — Stormwind's canals, Northshire's chapel for Brother Paxton, Goldshire's gold dust, Fargodeep Mine's low tunnels — errands strung together like beads while Cassidy stayed at his side nearly the whole way. She left him at last past one in the morning in the Trade District, and Rambleon stood a moment in Stormwind's lamplight before logging out, Westfall's dust still on him, Deadmines water still in his boots.
