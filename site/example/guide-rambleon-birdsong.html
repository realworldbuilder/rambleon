Rambleon Birdsong was eight levels into Azeroth when this record begins, and twenty-five when it last closes for the night. In between lie eighteen nights of a Night Elf hunter's early road — Teldrassil's groves, Darkshore's tides, Ashenvale's shadowed timber, the human roads of Elwynn and Westfall, the salt air of the Wetlands — walked once, imperfectly, the way any young hunter walks a world still larger than his understanding of it. What follows is that road exactly as his journal kept it: no shortcuts invented, no companions added, only what he did, where he did it, and what it cost him.

He began the night in Dolanaar, where the business of a young hunter was still mostly fetching and killing things that deserved it — Gnarlpine furbolgs thick in the woods, their gardeners and warriors and the great lumbering ursa among them. Hazardelf and Tiamaat kept him company for a stretch of it, which made the webwood lurkers and the corrupted furbolg camps feel less like a solitary chore. But by Lake Al'Ameth the company had thinned, and he found himself working alone among the timberlings and their sprouting kin, right down to the lasher sproutlings guarding the water's edge. He wrote it down plainly: alone at the lake, slaying timberlings. There was something in that solitude he didn't mind. By the time he'd put down Xethorr the Wicked and turned in Denalan's earth and the last of the timberling errands, level nine had settled on him like something earned rather than given.
