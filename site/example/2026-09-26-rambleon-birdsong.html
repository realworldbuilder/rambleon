Chapter 6 — The Ruins Read Their Own Story

Rambleon Birdsong · September 26, 2026 · 52m in Azeroth
StoryRecapJourney
← Chapter 5 — The Shores of Darkshore

Rambleon came back to Auberdine alone this morning, no Cassidy at his shoulder, no Banne trading blows beside him — just the quiet company of his own footsteps on ground that Darkshore had already made familiar. He struck out into the grasslands and found the old rhythm quickly enough: Moonstalker Runts and Thistle Bears falling to him one after another, the kind of steady, unremarkable work that asks nothing of a hunter but patience and a good eye.

The ruins of Ameth'Aran drew him in properly by midmorning, and there the day sharpened. Cursed Highborne, Wailing Highborne, Writhing Highborne — the fallen nobility of some older, sadder Darkshore — gave way to his bow and blade until the seventh Highborne relic came into his hands and the string of tools he'd been chasing was complete. It was in the middle of that work, almost incidentally, that his fifteenth year of adventuring arrived. He paused long enough to mark it with a picture among the broken stones, a small private acknowledgment rather than any kind of celebration, and then went back to what he was doing.

The ruins had more to say than plunder, though. He found the Lay of Ameth'Aran and read it, then found the fuller account of the city's fall and read that too, standing in the wreckage the words described — and something in learning a place's grief while standing in its bones settled into him quietly, the way that sort of thing does. When the reading was finished, so was the errand it belonged to. One last Moonstalker fell to him on his way out, almost an afterthought, and he turned north again through the Long Wash toward the coast.

Auberdine took him back in before noon. Whatever the Highborne relics had been for was settled and done, and in its place the town offered him fresh threads to pull — a warning posted about something called Murkdeep, a request concerning the sea's harvest, and word of a prospector who'd wandered off absent-minded and unaccounted for. He took all three, though the morning was nearly spent and he had no time left to chase them, and logged out with Darkshore's next chapter simply waiting, the way Ban'ethil Hollow once had.

It had been a solitary hour, no one falling in beside him the way Cassidy or Banne had on other nights, and there was a particular kind of loneliness in that — not unhappy, just quiet, a morning spent in his own company among stones that remembered more than he did.
