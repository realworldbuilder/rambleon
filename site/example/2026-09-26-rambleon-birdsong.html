Chapter 6 — Ameth'Aran's Last Word

Rambleon Birdsong · September 26, 2026 · 52m in Azeroth
StoryRecapJourney
← Chapter 5 — The Shores of Darkshore

He came back alone to the Long Wash and the ruined arches of Ameth'Aran, no Cassidy at his side this time, no Banne to trade blows with in the dark of Mist's Edge. Darkshore again, familiar under his boots after the mountain had already taught him its worst, and this morning it gave him something gentler: bears and moonstalkers thinning out along the coast, then the fallen highborne themselves, cursed and wailing and writhing, filling out the last measure of what the Tools of the Highborne had asked of him. Seven relics, gathered across who knew how many nights, and here at last the count came due.

Fifteenth level found him standing in Ameth'Aran itself, and Rambleon paused long enough to feel it — not the quiet pride of Bashal'Aran's crystal, worn thin by exhaustion, but something cleaner, earned in an hour of steady work. He let the screenshot catch him there before moving on, the way a person marks a threshold crossed alone. Twilight Vale, when he ventured into it this time, asked only that he read — the Lay of Ameth'Aran, then its grimmer counterpart, the Fall of Ameth'Aran, laid out on old stone for anyone who still cared what the highborne had been before they were cursed. He did care, enough to finish it, and The Fall of Ameth'Aran closed itself out behind him as neatly as the relic-quest had.

The walk back was short and unremarkable in the way mornings sometimes are after a hard stretch of nights — through the Long Wash, past a last moonstalker, and down into Auberdine with the sun still climbing. There Gwennyth's kind, or others like them, had more use for him: a note about a certain Murkdeep posted somewhere for the taking, fruit wanted from the sea, a prospector gone missing and worth the asking after. He took all three without hesitation, the way a hunter takes on work when the last job has just closed clean, and left the rest for whatever night came next.

He'd wondered, on the ride down from Darnassus days before, what waited on the far side of the Veiled Sea. Some of it, at least, he now knew the shape of — and none of it had needed to kill him twice to teach him.
