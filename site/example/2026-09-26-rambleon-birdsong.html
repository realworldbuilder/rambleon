Chapter 6 — The Lay of Ameth'Aran

Rambleon Birdsong · September 26, 2026 · 3h 20m in Azeroth
StoryRecapJourney

Ameth'Aran had not finished with him, nor he with it. Rambleon came back to the ruined city in late morning, the moonstalkers and thistle bears of Darkshore falling quickly along the way, and among the broken Highborne stones he ran down the last of the relics he needed — a seventh piece, the set complete. Fifteen years old now, he noted the milestone the way a hunter notes a good trail: without ceremony, just a small settling-in of pride, and pressed on to read what the ruins themselves had to say. The Lay of Ameth'Aran and the Fall of Ameth'Aran, both found among the wreckage, told him the city's own ending before he closed the matter out and turned back toward Auberdine.

There the harbor handed him a fresh handful of business — a wanted notice for something called Murkdeep, a favor concerning the sea's bounty, an absent-minded prospector's troubles — and he saw the Highborne relics through to their reward before the afternoon carried him elsewhere. It was well past two by the time Cassidy found him again in Auberdine, familiar as the tide line itself now, three nights running, and the two of them struck east together toward the Tower of Althalaxx.

The tower gave up its secrets grudgingly. He and Cassidy cleared it once, turned in the parchments they'd gathered, and went back in for more — Delmanis the Hated among those who fell — while the surrounding coast finally yielded its last unexplored corner, Darkshore entire now marked on his map. A sample drawn from the Cliffspring River closed another small errand before duty called him away entirely: back across the Veiled Sea to Rut'theran Village, a word delivered for Nessa, a short walk through Darnassus under its familiar lantern-light, and then home again to the coast.

Twilight Shore was where the evening turned hard. The Greymist murlocs there did not care for pride or momentum, and death found him again and again along that stretch of water — he counted eleven times before the night was through, and stopped counting how many times he'd waded back in. Company came and went in the thick of it: a Paladin named Caerwyn for a few minutes, a fellow Hunter named Reighna who helped him finally corner the wanted Murkdeep, and Elentria, a Paladin, briefer still. By the time the sea creatures' bones and a strange marked box had been carried back to Gwennyth in Auberdine, and the wanted poster torn down, Cassidy had turned for home as well, leaving Rambleon to close the ledger on a long, damp, stubborn afternoon.
