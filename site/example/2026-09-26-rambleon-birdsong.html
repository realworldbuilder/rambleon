Chapter 6 — Tools of the Highborne

Rambleon Birdsong · September 26, 2026 · 1h 01m in Azeroth
StoryRecapJourney

The morning found him back on the shores of Darkshore, where the mountain's business was finally winding down. Auberdine again, then the wilder country beyond, and by the time the sun was properly up he had put down a Moonstalker Runt and a Thistle Bear both, the easy proof that Ban'ethil Hollow's lessons had stuck with him. Ameth'Aran waited a little further on, its ruins thick with Cursed and Wailing and Writhing Highborne, and Rambleon worked through them with the steady patience the coast had been teaching him all week — until, between one kill and the next, his fifteenth year found him. He paused only long enough to have the moment set down in a picture, the way a man marks a milestone he means to remember, before the ruins pulled him back in.

There was reading to do among the fallen stones, too — the Lay of Ameth'Aran and its grim companion, the Fall, both worth the finding — and with the last word taken in, the tale of the Highborne's ruin closed itself out, the seventh and final relic finally in hand. A Moonstalker fell to him as he made his way clear of Twilight Vale, and then the Long Wash carried him quietly back toward Auberdine, the harbor town gathering him in with new business before the morning was through: word of a lurking creature called Murkdeep, a matter of fruit for the sea, an absent-minded prospector's troubles. Small errands, the kind a town hands a capable stranger without ceremony, and Rambleon took them up before there was any more of the day to spend.

The hours after that ran quiet — no note in his memory of what filled them, only that when he next looked up, the afternoon had gone long and Auberdine was around him once more. It was there, near three, that Cassidy found him, as she so often did now; not a stranger falling in for an hour but an old traveling companion picking up a thread that had run through two nights already, Darnassus and the Barrow Den and all of Darkshore behind them. They had barely fallen into step before the session's hour ran out, but even that brief joining felt like a continuation rather than a beginning, the kind of ease that only comes from having walked beside someone before.

It had been a short morning as these things went, but a full one — a level won without much fanfare, an old questline finally laid to rest, and Cassidy's familiar company waiting at the end of it like a lamp left lit in a window.
