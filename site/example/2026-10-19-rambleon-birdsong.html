Chapter 28 — Back to Southshore

Rambleon Birdsong · October 19, 2026 · 1h 10m in Azeroth
StoryRecapQuestsJourney
← Chapter 27 — A Quiet Return

Cassidy found him in the Trade District before the hour was even properly begun, and Rambleon felt the old ease of it settle over him at once — sixteen nights together now, going back to Aldrassil, and not a single one of them needing an introduction. They did not linger in Stormwind. The city had held him long enough the night before; tonight it was only a doorway.

What followed was less a walk than a long unspooling of the map itself. The Canals gave way to Cathedral Square and back again, and then the world opened wide — Elwynn's green hush past Crystal Lake and the Northshire vineyards, the black heat of the Burning Steppes with Blackrock looming over Draco'dar, the choking haze of Searing Gorge, the cold relief of Dun Morogh's hills around Kharanos and the Amberstill farms. He paused for pictures at nearly every threshold, as if trying to hold each place still for a moment before the road pulled him on. Wetlands opened onto the Great Sea, and the Great Sea closed again into Hillsbrad — the same ground he and Carrie and Aso and Horn had crossed coming the other way, only now run backward, toward Southshore and the unfinished business that had been waiting there since before his quiet night alone in the Trade District.

Southshore received him plainly. He let go of two old threads that no longer served him — Researching the Corruption, Twilight Falls — and picked up two new ones in their place, Crashing the Wickerman Festival and The Power of Pine, small promises of work still to be done. A hunter named Quentyn fell in beside him for a few minutes, the kind of brief company that comes and goes on a road like this one, gone again almost as soon as he'd arrived. Rambleon did not mind. Cassidy was still there, and that was the company that mattered.

The last stretch of the night took him in and out of the Western Strand and the mouth of Azurelode Mine, back and forth, the way a man circles something before deciding whether to go in. He did not go far inside. The hour was getting on, and there would be time enough for the mine's dark on another night. For now it was enough to have come all this way, Cassidy beside him, Southshore's business finally within reach instead of only remembered.
